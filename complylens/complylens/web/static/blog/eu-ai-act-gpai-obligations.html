EU AI Act GPAI Obligations: What AI Model Providers Must Do

The EU AI Act treats general-purpose AI (GPAI) models — foundation models like LLMs — separately from high-risk systems. If you train, fine-tune, or deploy a GPAI model for EU users, these obligations apply.

Who is a GPAI provider

A GPAI provider develops or trains a model that can perform a wide range of tasks (foundation models, large language models). A GPAI deployer integrates such a model into a product or service for EU users.

Practical steps for 2026

  1. Determine your role: provider, deployer, or both.
  2. Document your model/system: training-data summary, evaluation results, intended uses.
  3. Label AI-generated content in your product (watermark, metadata, visible label) — required from August 2026.
  4. Check systemic-risk tier: if your model exceeds 10^25 FLOPs cumulative training compute, the heavy duties apply.
  5. Prepare incident reporting for serious incidents involving EU users.

The December 2026 milestone

Following the Digital Omnibus, most Annex III high-risk provisions moved to 2027, but GPAI transparency and content-labelling obligations apply from August 2026 — and the Code of Practice framework continues to evolve. If you ship any AI product to EU users, the labelling and documentation work is due this year.
