Colorado AI Act: The Next U.S. AI Hiring Regulation

While NYC's Local Law 144 has dominated AI hiring compliance since 2023, Colorado's AI Act (SB 24-205) adds a new layer for employers hiring in Colorado — and it takes a different regulatory approach.

What the Colorado AI Act requires

  • High-risk systems: AI making "consequential decisions" — including employment, hiring, promotion, and termination — is classified as high-risk.
  • Impact assessments: developers and deployers must conduct and retain impact assessments covering known risks and mitigation measures.
  • Notice: consumers (including job applicants) must be told when a high-risk system is used for a consequential decision, and given the opportunity to review data and appeal.
  • Human review: a human must be able to review the decision and its basis.

How it differs from NYC LL144

Core obligationAnnual independent bias audit + public summaryImpact assessment + notice + appeal
EnforcementDCWP (city agency), per-day finesAttorney General (state), $50,000+ per violation
Who it coversNYC-based positionsColorado residents

Why this matters now

Most Colorado provisions take effect in 2026. The combination of attorney-general enforcement (higher penalties than NYC's per-day structure) and the requirement to assess and document AI hiring tools makes early preparation cheap insurance. The good news: the statistical analysis you prepare for NYC LL144 — selection rates and impact ratios — supports the Colorado impact assessment, since both turn on measuring how your tool treats protected groups.
