EU AI Act Readiness Kit

Product: ComplyLens EU AI Act Readiness Kit (starter edition) — 5 templates for AI system providers preparing for the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

Informational templates. Not legal advice. Verify obligations against the final text and your specific use case.

1. Applicability & Risk Classification Checklist

Work through this to classify your AI system and identify which obligations apply.

A. Is it an AI system under Art. 3(1)?
   [ ] Machine-learning model (supervised/unsupervised/reinforcement)
   [ ] Logic- or knowledge-based system (expert systems, statistical approaches)
   [ ] Receives input and generates output (predictions, content, recommendations, decisions)
   If none: the AI Act likely does not apply.

B. Is it PROHIBITED under Art. 5?
   [ ] Manipulative/deceptive techniques exploiting vulnerabilities
   [ ] Social scoring (public/private)
   [ ] Real-time biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces (law enforcement — narrow exceptions)
   [ ] Emotion inference in workplace/education
   If YES: the system may not be placed on the market. STOP.

C. Is it HIGH-RISK under Annex III (with the Digital Omnibus amendments)?
   [ ] Biometrics (remote identification, categorisation, emotion)
   [ ] Critical infrastructure management (safety components)
   [ ] Education & vocational training (admission, evaluation)
   [ ] Employment & worker management (recruitment, promotion, termination)
   [ ] Access to essential services (credit, insurance, healthcare, energy)
   [ ] Law enforcement (risk assessment, evidence evaluation, profiling)
   [ ] Migration & border control
   [ ] Administration of justice
   [ ] Democratic processes (disinformation/influence)
   If YES: full high-risk obligations apply (risk management, data governance,
   technical documentation, logging, transparency, human oversight, accuracy).

D. Is it a GPAI model under Art. 51?
   [ ] Trained with cumulative compute > 10^25 FLOPs (GPAI with systemic risk)
   [ ] Otherwise a general-purpose AI model
   If YES: transparency/summary obligations apply; systemic-risk tier adds more.

E. Does Art. 50 transparency apply?
   [ ] Chatbot / conversational AI interacting with humans
   [ ] Deepfake / synthetic content generation
   [ ] Emotion recognition or biometric categorisation
   If YES: disclose AI interaction / mark synthetic content.

Key dates: GPAI obligations applied Aug 2025; transparency and (post-Digital-Omnibus) high-risk obligations from 2 Aug 2026; remaining high-risk provisions from 2 Aug 2027. Confirm current deadlines before relying on them.

2. Risk Management System Template (Art. 9)

Provider: [company] | Date: [YYYY-MM-DD] | Reviewer: [name]

1. Intended purpose (Art. 3(12)):
   [Describe the system's intended use, environment, and end users.]

2. Reasonably foreseeable misuse:
   [List scenarios of misuse and how the system detects/limits them.]

3. Risk identification (hazard categories):
   | ID | Hazard | Scenario | Severity (1-4) | Likelihood (1-4) | Risk level |
   |----|--------|----------|----------------|------------------|------------|
   | R1 | [e.g. biased output] | [when...] | 3 | 2 | Medium |
   | R2 | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

4. Risk evaluation & mitigation:
   | ID | Mitigation measure | Residual risk | Evidence (test/record) |
   |----|--------------------|---------------|------------------------|
   | R1 | [e.g. balanced training data, threshold calibration, human review] | Low | [link to eval report] |

5. Acceptable residual risk statement:
   [Confirmed by responsible person that residual risks are acceptable for the intended purpose.]

6. Review cycle: [quarterly] | Next review: [date]

3. Technical Documentation Template (Art. 11 / Annex IV)

TECHNICAL DOCUMENTATION — [SYSTEM NAME] v[X]
Provider: [company] | Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]

1. General description:
   - Intended purpose, system type (ML/logic-based), version
   - Interaction with hardware/software environment
   - Inputs/outputs and modalities
   - How the system is used (deployment context, users)

2. Detailed description:
   - Architecture and development approach
   - Data: sources, collection, preprocessing, labelling, validation
   - Human oversight measures (Art. 14): who reviews, when, how
   - Expected accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity (Art. 15) and test results

3. Change management:
   - Version history and what changed
   - Re-evaluation triggers (data drift, major updates)

4. Conformity references:
   - Harmonised standards / common specifications used (or justification for alternative approach)
   - This document must be kept up to date and provided to national authorities on request.
