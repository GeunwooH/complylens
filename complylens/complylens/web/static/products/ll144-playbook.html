NYC Local Law 144 Compliance Playbook

Paid product — delivered after confirmed payment. This playbook condenses the verified research behind ComplyLens (63 validated claims, sources in the public research page) into a step-by-step compliance guide.

1. Does the law apply to you?

You are covered if you use an automated employment decision tool (AEDT) — a machine-learning-based screener, ranker, or scorer — for hiring or promotion decisions involving positions based in New York City, including remote roles open to NYC residents.

2. The three obligations

  • Annual bias audit: an independent bias audit completed within the last 12 months (selection rates, impact ratios, four-fifths assessment per DCWP Final Rules).
  • Public summary: a summary of the most recent audit posted publicly on your careers site.
  • Candidate notice: 10 business days' notice before AEDT use, including the option to request an alternative process.

3. Penalty reality check

Penalties run per day: $500 first violation, $500–$1,500 per day after, per tool. The Dec 2025 State Comptroller audit (2024-N-6) found 17 potential violations in 32 companies and concluded enforcement was ineffective — enforcement is tightening, and the exposure math is unchanged: six months non-compliant ≈ $90,000–$270,000.

4. Data you need for an audit

Exports from Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, or a CSV with candidate records: candidate ID, protected category (sex, ethnicity), and selection outcome or score.

5. What to do this week

  1. Inventory which tools qualify as AEDTs.
  2. Confirm your last audit date and public summary.
  3. Check candidate notice language and timing.
  4. Commission a current audit if any of the above is missing.
