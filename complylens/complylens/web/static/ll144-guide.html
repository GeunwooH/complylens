NYC Local Law 144 Bias Audit: The Complete 2026 Guide

Local Law 144 (LL144) requires New York City employers using automated employment decision tools (AEDTs) in hiring or promotion to obtain an annual independent bias audit, publish a summary, and notify candidates. Enforcement has shifted from paper-only to proactive investigations since January 2026, with the first fines issued. This guide covers everything you need.

Who is covered

You are covered if you use an AEDT — a machine-learning or statistical tool that substantially assists or replaces discretionary screening, evaluation, or ranking — for positions based in NYC, including remote roles open to NYC residents. Your company's headquarters location does not matter.

What the audit computes (methodology)

Per DCWP Final Rules (6 RCNY Subchapter T):

  • Selection rate: share of candidates selected per protected category (sex, ethnicity, and intersections).
  • Impact ratio: category selection rate ÷ highest selection rate. Below 0.80 (four-fifths rule) flags possible adverse impact.
  • Score-based metrics: for continuous-score tools, scoring rates above the median and score-based impact ratios.

Penalties and enforcement in 2026

Penalty structure (per-day, per-tool): $500 first violation; $500–$1,500 each subsequent day.

Enforcement status: The Dec 2025 State Comptroller audit (2024-N-6) found 17 potential violations in 32 companies. DCWP moved to proactive investigations in January 2026; the first fines were issued in late 2025/2026. Law firms advise expecting tighter enforcement through 2026.

FAQ

Does LL144 apply to remote roles? Yes — any NYC-based position, including remote roles open to NYC residents.

Is my ATS an AEDT? If it uses machine learning to screen, rank, or score candidates, almost certainly yes.

Can I do the audit myself? The audit must be performed by an independent auditor with no financial interest in the tool or employer.

What about Illinois/Colorado/California? Multi-state obligations are expanding (IL HB 3773, CO AI Act, CA ADMT). The same statistical analysis supports multiple jurisdictions.
